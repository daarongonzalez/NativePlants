## 1. The rebate database

### Layer one: the rules

What the Utah program says must be true, stored so we can check a plan against
it and show the gardener why.

```
rebate_programs        (exists; add) min_area_sqft, completion_months,
                       correction_days, inspection_blackout_start,
                       inspection_blackout_end, payee, rules_version

rebate_program_areas   program_id, community_name, water_supplier,
                       has_secondary_water, geom(MultiPolygon),
                       source_url, verified_at

rebate_rules           id, program_id, rules_version, code ("UT-C2"),
                       stage (application | site_visit | completion | paperwork),
                       source_text (word for word), plain_text,
                       check_type, parameters (jsonb), source_url,
                       verified_at, verified_by
```

Every rule gets one `check_type`:

- **plan_check**: we can work it out from the plan (200 sq ft minimum, 50%
  perennial cover, park strip height, 8 ft lawn widths, hardscape share,
  excluded materials)
- **owner_confirms**: only the owner knows (lawn alive at first visit, nothing
  started before approval, drip type and emitter rating, plants in the ground)
- **inspector_only**: only the final visit settles it (no leaks, filters
  visible, mulch depth)

`parameters` holds the numbers, so a rule change is a data edit, not a code
change. For example, `{"min_pct": 50, "excludes": ["tree_canopy", "annual", "bulb"]}`.

A rule change gets a new `rules_version`. Old checks keep pointing at the
version they were run against, so the record stays honest.

**Still needed from the program's own page:** the current amount per square
foot and cap (the landing page uses $2.00, checked October 2026), and the list
of participating communities.

**The plant sheet needs three new columns** for the coverage and park strip
rules: life cycle (perennial, annual, bulb), plant form (ground cover, shrub,
tree) and `stays_under_24in` (true only if no pruning is needed).

### Layer two: the tracker

A record of what happens to each plan, for our own reporting.

```
rebate_projects        id, site_id, program_id, area_kind
                       (front | back | park_strip), area_sqft,
                       remaining_lawn_min_width_ft, hardscape_pct,
                       created_at

rebate_checks          id, project_id, rules_version, overall
                       (likely | needs_changes | unknown), created_at

rebate_check_results   check_id, rule_id, result
                       (pass | warn | fail | owner_to_confirm),
                       measured_value, note

rebate_outcomes        project_id, stage (applied | site_visit_approved |
                       completed | final_passed | final_failed |
                       corrected | paid), reported_by (owner | program),
                       reported_at, note
```

**Where the results come from:**

- **Our own check:** recorded automatically every time a plan is checked.
- **The owner:** follow-up emails at each stage ask "did this pass?" One click
  per answer.
- **The program:** only if the water district agrees to share results. That is
  a partnership conversation, not a build task.

**What we will be able to report:** plans created, the share that pass our
check, the rules people miss most, how far people get from plan to payment,
and how often our check agreed with the inspector. That last one is the number
that earns trust with the water districts.

## 2. The address-to-plan flow

The landing page already has the pieces: two address forms, a rebate
calculator and a plot planner (`PlotPlanner.tsx`, built but not yet on the
page). The flow joins them into one path.

1. **Your address.** Enter it on the landing page.
2. **Confirm the spot.** A draggable pin.
3. **Your ground.** The site profile: zone, frost, soil, sun. Free, with no
   email needed. This keeps the V1 promise of value without signing up.
4. **The game.** Choose the area (front, back, park strip) and its size. Pick
   plants, watch the coverage meter fill towards 50%, and see the rebate
   estimate and the rule checklist update as you go. Every number shows its
   source.
5. **Get your plan.** Enter your email for the plan: the plant list, the rule
   checklist, what to do next and the program's deadlines.

**Before the email step can go live:**

- A privacy policy and terms of service (already on the V1 checklist)
- A way to send email
- Clear consent wording for the follow-up emails that feed the tracker

## 3. Blog, FAQ and About

The aim is to be the source an answer engine quotes when someone asks about
Utah's lawn rebate. That means pages that are specific, current, signed and
sourced.

- **About.** Who we are, who reviews the plants (a named person), where every
  number comes from, and how often we re-check it.
- **FAQ.** Rebate questions answered from the rules table, so the answers
  never drift from what the checker uses. Marked up as an FAQ page, with "last
  checked" shown on the page.
- **A rebate program page.** The Utah program in plain language: amount,
  rules, timing and who is eligible.
- **Blog, first six posts:**
  - How the Utah lawn rebate works, step by step
  - The 8 ft lawn rule, with drawings
  - What counts towards 50% plant cover
  - Drip irrigation that qualifies, and what does not
  - Park strip plants that stay under 24 inches
  - When to start: the seasons and the 12-month window

**Direction, 8 November 2026:** headings and pitch speak to any yard; Utah is
the worked example, not the only region.

**How they get built:** written as files in the repository and rendered by
the web app. No content system yet. Each page uses the Nativo components and
carries an author, a reviewer, a date and its sources.

## 4. Later: photo checks and the full service

Not in V1. What we do now so it is not a rewrite later:

- `check_type` leaves room for a fourth value, `photo_check`, for owner
  confirmations a photo could settle (emitter type, mulch depth, filters
  visible, plants in the ground).
- `rebate_outcomes` already runs from application to payment, which is the
  backbone of a service that handles every step.
- The program itself asks for photos of the existing lawn. When photos come
  in, that is the first use. The privacy and retention rules in
  [scope.md](scope.md) still apply first.
- Nursery and trade introductions sit on top of the same project record, and
  are Phase 4 in the [roadmap](roadmap.md).

## Built so far

Steps 1 to 3 are in the code. The plot planner is the game.

**The rebate database (`packages/db`, migration `0002_rebates`)**

- Tables: `rebate_programs`, `rebate_program_areas`, `rebate_rules`, and the
  tracker: `rebate_projects`, `rebate_checks`, `rebate_check_results`,
  `rebate_outcomes`. The tracker stores no address, email or user, only the
  plan shape, the results and a ZIP code.
- `data/rebates/ut-landscape-incentive.json` holds all 23 Utah rules, each in
  the program's own words with a plain version and who can check it. Load it
  with **Actions → Run ingestion → `rebates`**.
- The plant sheet has `life_cycle`, `plant_form` and `stays_under_24in`. The
  33 drafts are filled in as drafts.

**The check (`packages/engine/src/rebate.ts`)**

- Checks a planner layout against six rules: 200 sq ft minimum, 50% plant
  cover when grown, no lawn under 8 ft wide, hardscape limits, park strip
  height and materials that are not paid for. Every other rule is listed as
  "you confirm" or "inspector checks", never as passed.
- Cover is worked out from each plant's grown width, at 5 ft squares. It is
  an estimate, and the page says so.

**Visitor routes in the API (`apps/api/src/public.ts`)**, no sign-in:
address lookup, a profile built without saving, the program with its rules
and planner plants, and "check my plan", which checks again on the server and
records the result.

**The web flow (`apps/web`)**: `/start` (is this your yard?), `/report`
(your ground, with the sun question), and `/plan` (the plot planner and the
full rule list). The landing page's address boxes start the flow, and its
sample planner links to `/plan`. The web Worker reaches the API through a
service binding.
