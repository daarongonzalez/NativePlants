# Competitive Landscape

Status: draft, September 2026.

## Note on sourcing

`simplyscapes.com` is blocked by this environment's network proxy, so this
analysis is assembled from search results, their own help documentation as it
surfaced in search, and a third-party company profile. It has not been verified
by using the product. Anything below marked as a gap should be confirmed by
signing up for their free tier before we act on it.

## SimplyScapes

### What they are

Browser-based landscape *visualization* software. Upload a photo of your yard,
describe a change in plain language, and their AI redesigns the photo
realistically. You then refine by hand, dragging in photographed plants,
hardscape, and objects. The output is a picture and a shopping list, exportable
as a PDF.

### The facts we have

| | |
|---|---|
| Founded | 2021, Orem, Utah |
| Team | Roughly 4 people |
| Funding | None raised, per Tracxn |
| Plant library | 2,400+ plants |
| Filters | Hardiness zone, sun, water use, mature size |
| Free tier | Permanent, up to 3 active designs, full toolkit |
| Plus | $29.99/mo, or $25/mo billed yearly ($300/yr) |
| Enterprise | White-label, team workspaces, dedicated support |
| Acquisition | Running Google Ads |

### Who they actually serve

The site positions for "professionals and homeowners," but the revenue features
point at professionals: white-label branding, team workspaces, client proposals,
and the pitch about starting a design on desktop, refining on an iPad at the
client's house, and sending the PDF from the truck. The homeowner free tier
reads as top of funnel.

### Where we overlap

- The same homeowner, at the same moment, planning the same yard.
- A plant library filtered to the user's location.
- A shopping list at the end.

### Where we don't

**They start from aesthetics. We start from the ground.** Their input is a
photo. Ours is a soil survey. They filter on four axes — zone, sun, water,
mature size — and every one of them is a proxy. Nothing in their material
mentions soil pH, texture, drainage, or frost probability. "Climate-appropriate"
is carrying a lot of weight in their copy.

**Visualization is not viability.** They answer *what will this look like?* We
answer *what will survive here, and when do I plant it?* A homeowner needs both.
Theirs is the more satisfying question and by far the better demo. Ours is the
more defensible one.

**No native plant thesis.** 2,400 plants filtered by hardiness zone is a nursery
catalog, not a position on natives. No sign of ecoregion, native range, or
wildlife value. The Tallamy and Audubon audience — which is growing — is
unclaimed by them.

**A shopping list is not commerce.** Their list saves you trips to the garden
center. It does not transact, and there is no supply side to their product at
all. Our two-sided model has no equivalent in theirs.

**No time dimension.** A render is a single moment. Bloom succession across a
season, or planting dates tied to the gardener's own frost bands, are absent.

### What this changes for us

1. **Their pricing anchors ours.** $25–30/month for homeowner-facing garden
   software with a permanent free funnel. We no longer have to guess at the
   gardener subscription, and we can position above or below deliberately.

2. **We will lose the demo.** A photo-realistic render of your own house beats a
   soil report on first impression, every time. Phase 3 visualization may need
   to arrive sooner than planned, or Phase 1 needs a visual artifact of its own —
   even a simple bed diagram beats a table of numbers.

3. **Their gap is our Phase 1; our gap is their whole product.** They built
   layout and visualization and skipped the site profile. We are approaching
   from the opposite end of the same problem. Whoever reaches both first wins
   the homeowner.

4. **The nursery side is the wedge they cannot follow.** Four people, unfunded,
   five years in, now buying ads. That is a viable small business, not a company
   that is about to go sign nurseries and clean up inventory catalogs. That work
   is slow, relational, and unglamorous — which is exactly why it holds.

### What to watch

If they add real soil data, they close the advice gap quickly, because the
visualization is the harder half and they already have it. That is the single
competitive signal worth monitoring.
